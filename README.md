# AI Research & Source Verification

## Overview

This repository demonstrates the use of **Generative AI to support business research** while maintaining a strong focus on source verification, evidence quality, and responsible research practices.

The project covers the complete AI-assisted research workflow—from developing a focused research question and generating search strategies to verifying AI-generated claims, comparing sources, maintaining an evidence table, and developing evidence-based business insights.

> **AI can help you research. AI itself is not automatically the evidence.**

---

## Objectives

* Understand AI-assisted research and its applications.
* Convert broad business topics into focused research questions.
* Use Generative AI to support research planning.
* Generate relevant research subtopics and search keywords.
* Identify and differentiate primary and secondary sources.
* Evaluate sources based on credibility and relevance.
* Verify AI-generated claims, statistics, and citations.
* Identify outdated, unsupported, or misleading information.
* Maintain structured evidence tables.
* Develop business insights using verified information.
* Apply responsible AI practices in academic and business research.

| Research Question                                           | AI Prompt                                                                       |

| Guides the overall research                                 | Supports a specific research activity                                           |
| Example: How is AI changing customer service in e-commerce? | Example: Identify five subtopics and search keywords for this research question |

## Source Types

### Primary Sources

Original information from an organization or researcher.

Examples:

* Government reports
* Official datasets
* Company annual reports
* Regulatory publications
* Original research studies

### Secondary Sources

Sources that discuss, analyze, or interpret original information.

Examples:

* News articles
* Industry analysis
* Business publications
* Review articles

The usefulness of a source depends on whether it is **appropriate for the specific claim being made**.

---

## Source Evaluation Framework

Important sources are evaluated using five key questions:

| Criterion        | Question                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Authority**    | Who created or published the information?        |
| **Currency**     | Is the information current enough for the topic? |
| **Evidence**     | What evidence or data supports the claim?        |
| **Relevance**    | Does the source address the research question?   |
| **Verification** | Can important claims be confirmed independently? |

---

## Statistics & Claim Verification

AI-generated statistics should not be accepted without verification.

For example, if AI provides a claim such as:

```text
AI adoption increased productivity by 47%.
```

The researcher should check:

* What does the 47% measure?
* Which industry was studied?
* Which country or market?
* What year does the data represent?
* How was productivity measured?
* Who conducted the study?
* What was the sample size?
* Does the original source actually support the claim?

A precise statistic can still be misleading when its context is missing.

## Claim Verification Process

The project follows this process:

```text
AI-Generated Claim
        ↓
Request / Identify Source
        ↓
Locate Original Source
        ↓
Open & Read Source
        ↓
Find Claimed Evidence
        ↓
Check Context & Date
        ↓
Verify Claim
        ↓
Verified / Partially Verified / Not Verified
```

If a claim cannot be verified, it should **not be presented as an established fact**.

## AI-Generated Sources

Generative AI can sometimes produce:

* Non-existent research papers
* Incorrect authors
* Incorrect publication dates
* Wrong URLs
* Incorrect statistics attributed to real reports

Therefore:

> **Never cite a source only because AI mentioned it.**

The original source should be located and checked before citation.

## Evidence Table

An evidence table is used to organize and verify research findings.

| Research Claim                        | Source   | Publication Date | Verified? | Evidence / Notes         | Use in Report? |
| ------------------------------------- | -------- | ---------------- | --------- | ------------------------ | -------------- |
| AI is being used for customer support | Source 1 | 2026             | Yes       | Relevant evidence found  | Yes            |
| AI reduces service cost by 40%        | Source 2 | 2024             | No        | Claimed figure not found | No             |

The evidence table helps maintain a clear connection between **claims, sources, verification, and final research use**.

## Triangulation

For important claims, information can be compared across multiple reliable sources.

Example:

```text
Government / Regulatory Source
             +
Company Annual Report
             +
Academic / Industry Research
             ↓
     Cross-Checked Evidence
```

Comparing sources can help identify:

* Areas of agreement
* Differences in findings
* Limitations
* Contextual differences
* Stronger evidence

## Fact vs Interpretation vs Recommendation

Research should clearly distinguish between evidence and analysis.

### Fact

Information directly supported by evidence.

### Interpretation

The researcher's understanding of what the evidence may mean.

### Recommendation

A suggested action based on the evidence and interpretation.

```text
Evidence
   ↓
Interpretation
   ↓
Recommendation
```

Keeping these separate improves clarity and research integrity.

---

## Citation Practices

Citations help:

* Give credit to original sources.
* Allow readers to verify information.
* Improve research credibility.
* Avoid presenting another person's work as your own.

For each useful source, record:

* Author / Organization
* Title
* Publication date or year
* Publisher, where applicable
* Link or DOI
* Access date, if required

The final citation format should follow the citation style required by the institution or project.

---

## Paraphrasing & Citations

Changing the wording does **not** remove the need for citation.

```text
Original Evidence
       ↓
Understanding
       ↓
Paraphrasing in Own Words
       ↓
Citation
```

If the underlying idea or information comes from another source, the source should still be acknowledged.

## Research Integrity

The project follows responsible research practices:

* Do not invent sources.
* Do not invent statistics or data.
* Do not use fake citations.
* Verify important AI-generated claims.
* Do not copy research reports.
* Clearly identify uncertainty.
* Protect confidential information.
* Write analysis in your own words.
* Maintain responsibility for the accuracy of the final research.
